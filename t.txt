Following the RFET walkthrough, here is the summary for the second workstream: Data Analytics & Assessment.


WHAT THIS WORKSTREAM DOES

RFET tells us whether a risk factor is modellable. But modellable or not, the data behind it must be complete, accurate, and defensible. That is what this workstream delivers.

Every risk factor needs three things to produce a reliable capital number:
• Accurate history going back to 2007 for stress period calibration
• A justified proxy if direct data doesn't exist
• Clean data — no gaps, no outliers, no stale prices contaminating the calculation

If any of these are wrong, ES is wrong, PLA breaks, backtesting produces false exceptions, and capital becomes unstable. This workstream finds the problems, builds the tools to fix them, and then fixes them.


HOW IT IS STRUCTURED

The work has three layers.


LAYER 1 — FIND EVERY RISK FACTOR AND IDENTIFY THE GAPS

Before fixing anything, we need to know what we have. Today, market data objects live in FO pricing systems (Vasara, Mercury, SPG). Risk factors live in risk engines (EMR, MARS). SA risk factors live in a separate engine. PLA risk factors sit in another layer. These systems were not built to talk to each other.

This layer catalogs everything, maps MDOs to risk factors, and classifies every relationship into five categories:
• Correctly mapped — no action
• Not mapped but should be — need to build a new risk factor
• Not in scope — pricing input, not market risk
• FO/Risk disconnect — Front Office uses one representation, Risk uses a different one
• Future needs — risk factors required by upcoming model migrations

The output is a prioritised gap register ranked by capital impact. That register drives every investment decision downstream.


LAYER 2 — BUILD SIX FOUNDATIONAL CAPABILITIES

Each foundation is a reusable tool that the team operates with:

1. Proxy framework — the rules, methodologies, and registry so every proxy is justified, validated under stress, and documented with full lineage. Three milestones: define principles with Risk Analytics, implement per asset class (IR, FX, Credit, Equity, Commodity), stand up BAU monitoring.

2. Data quality engine — a four-tier detection system. Tier 1: basic checks (gaps, stale prices, outliers). Tier 2: statistical anomalies (vol regime shifts, distribution changes). Tier 3: peer-group checks (correlation breakdowns, proxy drift). Tier 4: ML-based intelligent triage that learns which alerts are real vs. noise. Every alert is linked to its capital impact so we remediate by materiality.

3. Remediation tools — approved methods to fix problems. Gap-filling, outlier treatment, adjustments, overrides — all preserving tail risk, all auditable, all validated by Model Risk Management before use.

4. RF onboarding process — a repeatable pipeline for new risk factors: vendor assessment → selection → data onboarding → history methodology → generation methodology → proxy assignment → Tech build → VaR impact → backtesting → NTE/TSV → MRM approval → production. Build the process once, execute many times.

5. Carry removal — separate expected income (carry) from genuine market risk in the return distributions. Carry biases ES upward, distorts PLA, and misleads backtesting. Three scenarios depending on data availability: curve interpolation for rates, theta approximation for vol surfaces, statistical estimator as fallback.

6. History extension — get every risk factor's data back to 2007 for stress period coverage. Three paths: buy it from a vendor, construct it from available raw data, or extend via proxy. Handle benchmark transitions (LIBOR→SOFR). Each method validated by Risk Analytics and MRM.


LAYER 3 — USE THE TOOLS TO DELIVER PRODUCTION-READY DATA

This is the execution layer. The foundations are the how. This layer is the doing.

The team assesses every risk factor across all six dimensions, classifies it into an action category, and executes:
• Needs proxy work → apply Foundation 1
• Needs history work → apply Foundation 6
• Needs DQ remediation → apply Foundation 3
• Needs carry stripping → apply Foundation 5
• Needs full onboarding → run Foundation 4 pipeline
• Blocked → escalate, track, unblock

Every action is prioritised by capital impact. The result is validated against ES, PLA, and backtesting. Senior management signs off. Data goes to production.

Then ongoing monitoring: daily dashboard, weekly cross-foundation triage, monthly progress reporting, quarterly reconciliation, annual deep audit.


HOW THIS CONNECTS TO RFET

• This workstream defines the risk factor universe that RFET tests
• RFET's classification tells this workstream which risk factors are most capital-critical to fix — if something is non-modellable because of a weak proxy, it becomes this team's top priority
• This workstream delivers the quality-assured data into ES/VaR engines. RFET determines which ES bucket each risk factor goes into (IMCC vs. SES). Together they determine the final capital number.


WHAT CAN GO WRONG

1. Siloed systems — MDOs, risk factors, SA factors, and PLA factors all live in different systems that don't talk to each other. If the mapping between them is incomplete, risk factors fall through the cracks.

2. Proxy quality under stress — proxies that work in normal markets may fail during stress. Our 2007–2009 stress period calibration depends on proxy data from that era. If the proxy didn't behave like the actual risk factor during GFC, our stress ES is wrong.

3. History depth — not every risk factor has data back to 2007. Vendor sourcing takes time and money. Constructed histories must preserve tail behaviour. Proxy-based extensions must be validated against stress periods.

4. Carry contamination — if carry is not stripped, ES is biased upward, PLA is distorted (HPL includes carry, RTPL may not), and backtesting exceptions are misleading.

5. DQ issues hiding in plain sight — a stale price or a slowly drifting proxy doesn't trigger an alarm until it causes a PLA failure or a backtesting exception. By then the damage is done. The DQ engine must be predictive, not reactive.

6. Change control — every proxy swap, every history correction, every data treatment changes ES. Without governance, these changes appear as unexplained capital volatility.

7. Foundation tools without execution — six beautifully designed frameworks sitting on a shelf deliver zero value. The execution layer (Layer 3) is what actually moves risk factors from broken to production-ready.


KEY DEPENDENCIES

• Risk Analytics (RA) — co-develops every methodology (proxy, carry, DQ, remediation, history extension)
• Front Office — provides MDO catalogs, pricing model context, confirms FO/Risk disconnects
• Technology — builds production systems from Quant specifications (two tracks: Vasara and MCRM)
• Model Risk Management — validates and signs off every methodology before production use
• Capital Tech — runs parallel ES calculations, confirms IMCC/SES consumption
