RFET is one of the most critical IMA workstreams because it is the gatekeeper for capital routing. It decides which risk factors go into ES/IMCC and which fall into SES, directly impacting capital aggregation,
NMRF charges, and the final capital number.

This is a completely new buildout to create the end-to-end framework that classifies every risk factor as Modellable or Non-Modellable.

1. Quantitative Test — Observation Count
Counts valid Real Price Observations for each risk factor. Before counting, we must source RPOs, store and normalize them, and map them to the correct internal risk factors. The hardest part is the mapping.

2. Qualitative Test — Defensibility
Qualitative — all six tests listed with one-line plain-English explanations, then the critical insight: it's not black or white, but it's the most important test because 
it exposes everything — proxy weaknesses, derivation gaps, methodology drift. Every weakness it finds creates work
This involves judgment, so we need clear principles, rules, and governance that Tech can automate.

The regulatory standards must be hashed out through collaboration across Risk, Quants, FO, Data, and Technology. We need to define the principles, rules, and evidence required to pass or fail each standard check, then translate those rules into automation, controls, dashboards, and governance.


The main body of work is to build:

RPO data management and ingestion
RPO-to-RF mapping engine
RFET classification engine
Monitoring dashboards
BAU governance and reclassification controls

Modellable RFs pass both tests and flow into IMCC, which is more capital-efficient.
Non-Modellable Type A pass the qualitative test but fail the observation-count test.
Non-Modellable Type B fail the qualitative test, regardless of observation count, and are the most punitive.
