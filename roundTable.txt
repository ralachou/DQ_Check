## EY Roundtable — C-Suite Lessons Learned on FRTB IMA

### Executive headline

**IMA is not a model approval exercise. It is a capital operating model.**

### C-suite wisdom points

1. **Capital relief is earned, not assumed.**
   IMA can reduce capital only if desks pass RFET, PLA, backtesting, and evidence standards.

2. **Getting approved is not the finish line. Staying approved is the real cost.**
   Daily PLA/backtesting performance, quarterly RFET, and validation evidence create a permanent operating burden.

3. **NMRF can turn IMA into a capital penalty.**
   A desk can be approved for IMA but still carry high capital if key risk factors fail modellability.

4. **The long pole is not the model. It is data, RFET, infrastructure, tooling, and evidence.**
   Banks spent the most time on data sourcing, RPO mapping, IT infrastructure, tooling, and repeatable controls.

5. **Early movers used IMA to modernize the bank.**
   Some banks started years ago and used the program to clean data, modernize infrastructure, increase compute power, and build reusable capital analytics.

6. **Scope decisions made today become capital outcomes tomorrow.**
   The first buildout will define which desks, products, and risk factors are in scope, out of scope, modellable, or capital-punitive.

7. **Readiness is not only capital impact. It is passability.**
   A desk should be prioritized only if it has capital benefit, strong PLA/backtesting prospects, clear RFET evidence, and manageable operational complexity.

8. **Desk structure is a capital design choice.**
   Granularity matters. Some banks adjusted desk structure because PLA, backtesting, and capital outcomes are assessed at desk level.

9. **IMA requires judgment, not perfection.**
   The best approach is not always the most theoretically pure model. It is the approach that is sufficient, capital-efficient, defensible, and operationally feasible.

10. **If it cannot be rerun, evidenced, and defended, it will not scale.**
    Regulators and internal validation will expect empirical evidence, repeatable criteria, acceptance thresholds, and clear governance.

### Practical takeaway for leadership

The resource ask should not be framed as “IMA compliance spend.”
It should be framed as **capital infrastructure investment**.

We need funding for:

* End-to-end SA vs IMA capital impact assessment.
* Modellable vs NMRF capital simulation.
* RFET/RPO data ingestion, storage, mapping, bucketing, and counting.
* PLA and backtesting analytics.
* Evidence generation and validation workflows.
* Desk/product/risk-factor scope decision tooling.
* Sustainable BAU controls to remain eligible after approval.

### Bottom line

**IMA is a strategic capital option, but only if we build the data, tooling, evidence, and operating model to support it
Otherwise, we may get approval but still carry higher capital through NMRF, PLA amber/red outcomes, backtesting exceptions, or fallback to SA.**
